# Foo — Version 1.1.8

Copy-ready App Store strings, organized by language. This release fixes the two things users complain about (adding food is hard, no recipes out of the box) and makes Foo do what its subtitle promises: help you use food before it expires.

**Limits:** App Name ≤ 30 · Subtitle ≤ 30 · Keywords ≤ 100 bytes · Promotional text ≤ 170 · Description ≤ 4000 · Screenshot title ≤ 16 · Screenshot subtitle ≤ 32

---

## Why these changes

Last 30 days to Oct 29, 2026: 2.47K impressions (+152%), 106 product page views (+71%), 21 first-time downloads (+91%), 1.1% conversion (daily average, -21%). Only 4% of impressions open the product page, while 20% of page views download. So the name change in 1.1.6 is bringing people in, and what they see first (icon, subtitle, first screenshots) is the weak spot. That's why 1.1.8 keeps the name and changes those.

| What a new user met in 1.1.7 | What it does to the stats | 1.1.8 |
|---|---|---|
| Empty book and empty inventory after onboarding | Nothing to do on day 1 | 12 built-in recipes; onboarding stocks the kitchen |
| A 6-field form per grocery, with a ruler for amounts (1000 g = 1000 ticks) | The top user complaint | Tap everything you bought and add it all at once |
| Recipes could only use food already in the pantry | Writing a first recipe meant leaving to add food | Any ingredient can be picked while writing the recipe |
| Italian, German, Spanish and French iPhones saw an English UI unless they found the picker on the last page of the book | Likely costs non-English users; the translated store pages promise a translated app | Follows the device language |
| Expiry alerts were always in English | Weak nudge in every other language | Translated |
| "Continue for free" on the onboarding paywall ignored taps (seen on iPhone 18 Pro; other sizes not checked) | Feels like being trapped on the paywall | Fixed |
| The "Steps" back button in the recipe editor ignored taps | Stuck on a step while writing a recipe | Fixed |
| Nothing tracked waste, though the paywall and Summary promised "Food Waste Insights" | The app's core promise wasn't measurable, and a claim App Review could question | A waste log with one-tap prompts on expired food, plus Pro stats |
| Notification permission asked out of nowhere, then up to two alerts per food, with no way to act on them | People say no, or turn notifications off, and a retention tool is lost | A page that explains and previews reminders first; one reminder a day at most, each opening the recipe that uses the food |
| Rating prompt only for Pro users with 5+ recipes | With 0 purchases, almost nobody was ever asked, so few ratings | Asked right after the 1st and 5th meal is cooked, free or Pro |

**On the goal:** reducing food waste is a good goal, and it's what makes Foo different from other recipe apps. But people search for "what can I cook with what I have" and "expiry tracker", not "food waste". So the store copy now leads with the concrete benefit, and the waste angle supports it.

## App Store Connect checklist

- [ ] **Deploy the CloudKit schema before release.** `RecipeModel` has a new `catalogID` field, `FoodConsumptionModel` has a new `rescued` field and `FoodWasteModel` is a new record type. In the CloudKit Console, deploy the Development schema to Production for `iCloud.giusscos.SpinFood`, after running a development build signed into iCloud so the field exists there. Skipping this can break iCloud sync for recipes.
- [ ] Note the current numbers: impressions, conversion, Day-1 / Day-7 / Day-28 retention, ratings count. Compare after 4 weeks on 1.1.8. Retention is the one to watch for the reminders; the app has no analytics, so how many people turn them on isn't measured.

- [ ] Archive and upload the 1.1.8 build, attach it to the version and submit for review. The new icon ships with the build.
